# PART I: THE NAME

## Arran McIlroy — A Name Written in Gaelic Before It Was Written in English

---

## 1. "ARRAN" — Árann / Arainn

### The Word Itself

The name **Arran** is a direct cognate of **Árann** (Old Irish) / **Arainn** (Middle Irish), the ancient name for the **Aran Islands** (*Oileáin Árann*) — the three limestone islands that sit at the mouth of Galway Bay, County Galway, Ireland.

This is not a loose association. The name is the same word. Whether given consciously after the islands or inherited through Gaelic naming tradition, **Arran** carries the Aran Islands in its syllables.

### Etymology

The most widely accepted scholarly etymology connects *Árann* / *Arainn* to a pre-Gaelic or early Celtic root meaning **"ridge"** or **"elevated place"**, related to the Old Irish *ard* (height) and possibly to a Proto-Celtic *\*arano-* or *\*areno-*. The Aran Islands are, geologically, exposed limestone ridges — the visible spine of the Burren formation extending into the Atlantic.

T.F. O'Rahilly (*Early Irish History and Mythology*, 1946) suggested the name may belong to an even older, **pre-Indo-European substrate** — a name so ancient it predates the Celtic languages entirely, surviving from the earliest human naming of the Irish landscape.

The Scottish **Isle of Arran** (Arainn in Scottish Gaelic) shares the identical name and etymology, reflecting the deep Gaelic continuum between Ireland and Scotland through the kingdom of Dál Riata.

**Key reference:** Edmund Hogan, *Onomasticon Goedelicum* (1910) — the standard reference for early attestations of Irish place-names — records *Arainn* in its earliest manuscript appearances.

### What the Aran Islands ARE in Gaelic Tradition

The Aran Islands are not merely a place. They are a **threshold**.

**Geographically:** They are the last habitable land before 3,000 miles of open Atlantic. They sit at the western edge of Europe — the *imeall* (edge, margin), the boundary between the known world and the unknown.

**Mythologically:** In Gaelic tradition, the west is the direction of the **Otherworld**. *Tír na nÓg* (the Land of the Young), *Mag Mell* (the Plain of Joy), *Emain Ablach* (the Apple-Island) — all lie somewhere beyond the western sea. The Aran Islands sit on this threshold. To stand on the cliffs of Inis Mór is to stand at the doorway between this world and the next.

**In the Lebor Gabála Érenn:** The Aran Islands are connected to the **Fir Bolg** — the fourth wave of invaders. After the Fir Bolg were defeated by the Tuatha Dé Danann at the First Battle of Mag Tuired (Moytura, in the Galway/Mayo borderlands), they retreated to the Aran Islands and to Connacht. The great stone fort of **Dún Aonghasa** (Dún Aengus) on Inis Mór is named for **Aengus mac Úmhór**, a Fir Bolg king. The fort sits on a 100-metre cliff above the Atlantic — literally on the edge between land and void, between the known and the unknown.

**In the seanchaí tradition:** The Aran Islands were one of the last strongholds of the living Irish-language storytelling tradition. Séamus Ó Duilearga and the Irish Folklore Commission collected extensively from the islands. J.M. Synge spent four summers there (1898–1902) and wrote *The Aran Islands* (1907), describing a community where the old tales were still alive. Liam O'Flaherty, born on Inis Mór in 1896, became one of the great Irish novelists. The islands are where the seanchaí tradition held longest against the tide of anglicisation.

**In the monastic tradition:** **St. Enda (Éinne) of Aran** (d. c. 530 CE) founded one of the earliest and most influential monasteries in Ireland on Inis Mór. Aran became a seedbed of Irish Christianity — St. Ciarán of Clonmacnoise and possibly St. Columba studied there. The islands were simultaneously a centre of the old pagan world (the Fir Bolg forts) and the new Christian world (the monasteries). They bridged both.

### What This Means for the Name "Arran"

To carry the name **Arran** is to carry in two syllables:

- The **threshold between worlds** — the liminal edge of Europe
- The **Fir Bolg retreat** — the defeated invaders who became part of the Irish story
- The **seanchaí tradition** — the last stronghold of the living Gaelic word
- The **monastic bridge** — where pagan Ireland and Christian Ireland met
- The **geological foundation** — bare limestone ridge, the exposed bone of the land

And critically: the Aran Islands sit in **Galway Bay** — directly off the coast of the subject's own ancestral Galway heartland. A person named Arran with roots in Galway is named after the islands that guard his own ancestral coastline.

## 2. "McILROY" — Mac Giolla Ruaidh

### The Gaelic Construction

**McIlroy** is an anglicisation of the Irish and Scottish Gaelic patronymic **Mac Giolla Ruaidh**. Every element is Gaelic:

| Element | Gaelic | Meaning |
|---------|--------|---------|
| **Mc / Mac** | *Mac* | "son of" |
| **Il / Gil** | *Giolla* | "servant, devotee, follower" (originally "youth, lad") |
| **Roy / Roe** | *Ruaidh* | "of the red(-haired) one" — genitive of *ruadh* (red) |

**Full meaning: "Son of the servant of the red one."**

### The Significance of "Giolla"

The element *Giolla* (earlier *Gilla*) is one of the most important name-elements in the Gaelic world. It was **enormously productive** in Irish and Scottish Gaelic name-formation, appearing in two distinct categories:

**Ecclesiastical/devotional names** — where *Giolla* means "devotee of" or "servant of" a saint:
- *Mac Giolla Chríost* → McIlchrist ("son of the servant of Christ")
- *Mac Giolla Pádraig* → Fitzpatrick / Kilpatrick ("son of the servant of Patrick")
- *Mac Giolla Íosa* → McAleese ("son of the servant of Jesus")
- *Mac Giolla Eoin* → McIlhone ("son of the servant of John")

**Secular names** — where *Giolla* means "servant" or "follower" of a chief or lord:
- *Mac Giolla Ruaidh* → McIlroy ("son of the servant of the red one")

In both cases, the *Giolla* element signals a **relationship of dedicated service** — a binding between a person and a higher authority, whether sacred or secular. The name-bearer's ancestor defined himself not by his own attributes but by **whom he served**.

### The Significance of "Ruaidh" (Red)

*Ruadh* / *Ruaidh* means "red" or "red-haired" in Irish. As a personal epithet, it was one of the most common descriptors in Gaelic naming — applied to chieftains, warriors, and kings. The "red one" (*an Ruadh*) to whom the original *Giolla Ruaidh* pledged service would have been a chief or lord distinguished by red hair or a ruddy complexion.

Red hair itself carries deep resonance in Gaelic tradition. Several major figures bear the epithet:
- **Ruaidhrí** (Rory) — "red king," the name of the last High King of Ireland, Ruaidhrí Ua Conchobair (Rory O'Connor, d. 1198), who was **King of Connacht**
- Red is associated with the fiery, the warrior, the untameable

### Regional Origins

The McIlroy surname is historically concentrated in **Ulster** — particularly Counties Antrim, Down, Fermanagh, and Tyrone. The sept's heartland lies in the north of Ireland.

However, the *Giolla*-name pattern itself is pan-Gaelic, appearing across Ireland and Scotland. And the historical fluidity of the Gaelic world — with constant movement between Ulster, Connacht, Scotland, and beyond — means that lineage connections cross provincial boundaries.

### The Full Name Decoded

**Arran McIlroy** in Gaelic reads as:

> **Árann Mac Giolla Ruaidh**
>
> *"[He of the] Threshold-Ridge, Son of the Servant of the Red One"*

Or, expanding the resonances:

> *"The one named for the islands at the edge of the world, descended from a line that defined itself by devoted service, in the shadow of a red-haired lord."*

This is not an English name. It is a Gaelic name wearing English clothes. Every syllable — Árann, Mac, Giolla, Ruaidh — is Irish.

## 3. THE NAME AND THE MILESIAN PATTERN

Here is what makes the name structurally significant within the *Lebor Gabála* framework:

**The Milesians named Ireland.** When the Sons of Míl landed, the poet **Amergin** encountered three sovereignty goddesses — Banba, Fódla, and **Ériu** — and promised Ériu that the island would bear her name. The act of **naming** was the act that legitimised the Milesian claim. Not the sword. The word.

**Arran McIlroy carries Ireland's names in his own name.** *Árann* — the name of Ireland's threshold-islands, the edge where the Gaelic world meets the Atlantic unknown. *Mac Giolla Ruaidh* — a patronymic constructed entirely in the grammar of Gaelic kinship: "son of the devoted servant." The name doesn't just reference Gaelic tradition. It is **composed in** Gaelic tradition.

**The Milesians were born in the east and named in the west.** They came from Scythia and Egypt, but they received their defining names — the names of the land — only when they arrived in Ireland. Arran McIlroy was born in Riyadh, but his name speaks only of Ireland: the western islands, the Gaelic kinship structure, the old language beneath the anglicisation.

The pattern holds: **eastern birth, western name**.
