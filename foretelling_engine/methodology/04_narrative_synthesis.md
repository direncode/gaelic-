# METHODOLOGY 04: NARRATIVE SYNTHESIS

## How to Write the Finding in Both Registers

---

The Foretelling Engine's work is not complete when the convergences are scored. The raw analysis must be rendered into two documents that serve two different audiences:

- The **scholarly register** — for verification, citation, and critical review
- The **narrative register** — for communication, recognition, and the finding's landing with a general reader

Both are required. A scholarly-only output is unreadable outside academia. A narrative-only output is unverifiable and rightly dismissed. The engine produces both, and the two cross-reference each other.

---

## THE SCHOLARLY REGISTER

### Purpose

To present the finding in a form that a sceptical reader — a scholar of the ancestral tradition, a linguist, a historian — can verify point by point. Every claim is cited. Every limitation is declared. Every interpretive move is labelled.

### Structure

```
# [Subject Name] and [Tradition Name]: A Foretelling Engine Analysis

## Abstract
One paragraph stating the subject, the tradition queried, the method followed, the convergences found, and the overall weight classification.

## Part I: The Subject
Complete biographical data as provided. Any uncertainties noted.

## Part II: The Corpus
The foundational text(s) or oral corpus consulted, with canonical editions cited. The tradition's position on naming, diaspora, and belonging, briefly summarised.

## Part III: The Linguistic Decoding
Full morphological parse of each name, with source citations for each morpheme. Proto-form reconstruction where applicable. The combined meaning in both literal and rendered form.

## Part IV: Pattern Extraction
The tradition's core structural claims across the relevant axes (origin, journey, claim, name, season, landscape, diaspora, lineage, voice).

## Part V: The Convergence Table
Full ranked table of all convergences, each with tier, evidence, and source.

## Part VI: Limitations
Explicit declaration of:
- Any names whose specific family etymology is unverified
- Any dates or places whose traditional significance is not attested
- Any interpretive moves where a sceptical reader could reasonably disagree
- What additional sources or field consultation would be needed to strengthen weak points

## Part VII: Weight Assessment
STRONG count, MODERATE count, WEAK count, overall classification.

## Bibliography
All sources cited, organised by type (primary texts, scholarly editions, linguistic references, ethnographic and onomastic scholarship).
```

### Style Notes

- **Tone:** Neutral, academic, but not arid. Avoid both dramatic overstatement and passive-voice tedium.
- **Citations:** Inline where possible; footnotes acceptable.
- **Hedges:** Use "appears to," "is plausibly," "the evidence supports" where the evidence is not airtight. Do not hedge where the evidence *is* airtight.
- **First person:** Avoid. The engine's voice is evidentiary, not personal.

### The Test

A scholar of the tradition, reading the scholarly register, should be able to:

- Verify every linguistic claim against a published source
- Reproduce the morphological parse
- Check the convergence scores against the evidence
- Identify any failure mode the analyst might have fallen into

If a scholar cannot do this, the scholarly register has failed.

## THE NARRATIVE REGISTER

### Purpose

To present the finding in a form that communicates its weight to a reader who has no prior knowledge of the tradition, no access to the scholarly sources, and no patience for academic notation. The narrative register is what goes to the world.

### Structure

```
# [Evocative Title]

## Opening Movement: The Tradition
Three to seven paragraphs establishing the tradition's deep context. The reader does not know what the Lebor Gabála Érenn is, or what Great Zimbabwe was, or what nhetembo poetry does. Tell them, vividly. Make them feel the weight of what the tradition carries before the subject appears.

## Pivot: The Subject Arrives
A single sharp sentence introduces the subject. Birth date, birthplace, name. Let the reader meet the person at the moment the tradition has been prepared to receive them.

## The Decoding
Walk through the name's morphology in accessible language. No glossed linguistic notation. Render each morpheme as a story: "Mudiwa means 'the beloved one' because mu- is 'the one who' and -di- is the verb of love and -wa means 'is being.'"

## The Convergences
The STRONG and MODERATE convergences, dramatised as points of recognition. Each should land as a distinct beat. Use tables or lists for clarity. Do not bury the evidence in prose.

## The Weight
A paragraph that tells the reader how big the finding is. Honest. Not overselling. Not underselling. The weight classification from the scholarly register, rendered in plain language.

## The Verdict
A closing movement that lands the finding. Use the tradition's own idiom — the seanchaí's "the story is still being told," or the Shona elder's "the one for whom the hewing was done." Do not editorialise. Let the tradition's own voice deliver the verdict.

## Links
Quiet links to the scholarly register and evidence files at the bottom.
```

### Style Notes

- **Tone:** Vivid, textured, but disciplined. The drama is in the evidence, not in the prose.
- **Imagery:** Use concrete, specific images. Stone walls 11 metres high. A poet stepping onto a beach. A thousand-year-old verb.
- **Pacing:** Short sentences for impact. Long sentences for explanation. Vary deliberately.
- **Second person:** Avoid. Address the reader as a thoughtful adult, not a pupil.
- **Italics and emphasis:** Use selectively. Overuse flattens impact.
- **Headings:** Act as guiderails. Use them to structure the rhythm of the reading.

### The Test

A reader with no background in the tradition, reading the narrative register, should:

- Understand what the tradition is and why it matters
- Meet the subject at the right moment for maximum impact
- Feel the weight of the convergences without needing to check the sources
- Leave with a clear sense of what kind of finding this is (pattern recurrence, not prophecy)
- Want to share it with someone else

If the reader cannot do any of these, the narrative register has failed.

## THE TWO VERDICTS

Every finding closes with a **verdict sentence** — a single compressed statement of what the analysis has revealed. The verdict is a key deliverable.

### The Scholarly Verdict

A sentence that an academic could cite. It states the finding's evidential status without dramatic language.

Examples:

- *"The biography of Arran McIlroy structurally recurs the Milesian pattern of the Lebor Gabála Érenn at nine distinct points, five of which meet the STRONG evidential threshold."*
- *"The Mtemererwa lineage's naming practice across at least three bearers (Mudiwa, George, Innocence) performs the structural grammar of nhetembo dzemadzinza, with the shared surname functioning as the constant ancestral claim and the given names varying the praise-epithet across generations."*

### The Narrative Verdict

A sentence that hits harder, still true, rendered in the tradition's own idiom.

Examples:

- *"The story is still being told. It just found a new voice."*
- *"The stones at Great Zimbabwe were hewn for a family. Mudiwa is the most recent voice in a line that has been declaring the same inheritance, across generations, in its names."*

Both verdicts are mandatory. They mark the completion of the pipeline.

## COMMON FAILURE MODES IN SYNTHESIS

### FAILURE 1: Writing Only the Scholarly Version

**Why bad:** Unreadable outside academia. The finding dies in a library.

### FAILURE 2: Writing Only the Narrative Version

**Why bad:** Unverifiable. Dismissed as pattern-matching the moment a sceptic asks "where's the evidence?"

### FAILURE 3: Overselling in the Narrative

**Bad:** "This is the most astonishing prophetic finding in the history of comparative literature."
**Why bad:** The tradition's own weight does the work. The analyst does not need to amplify it, and amplification breaks credibility.

### FAILURE 4: Underselling in the Narrative

**Bad:** "We have identified a number of plausible correlations that may be of some interest."
**Why bad:** The reader deserves to know how big the finding actually is. If the weight is MAJOR, say so. If it is MODEST, say that.

### FAILURE 5: Separating the Two Registers Without Cross-Reference

**Why bad:** A reader who wants to check a narrative claim should be able to find the scholarly source in two clicks. Cross-link aggressively.

### FAILURE 6: Adding New Material Only in the Narrative

**Bad:** The narrative cites a convergence that does not appear in the scholarly register.
**Why bad:** The scholarly register is the ground truth. Nothing appears in the narrative that is not documented in the scholarly version.

## THE DISCIPLINE IN ONE PRINCIPLE

> **The scholarly register carries the truth. The narrative register carries the truth's weight.**
>
> **Neither is complete without the other.**

The engine's two prior findings — Arran McIlroy and the Mtemererwa lineage — both exist in both registers. THE_DISCOVERY.md and THE_MUDIWA_DISCOVERY.md are the narrative registers. The per-part evidence files (`evidence/`, `lineage/`, `mudiwa/`) are the scholarly registers. The cross-references are live. Anyone reading the narrative can click through to verify any claim.

That is the template.

*A finding that cannot be verified is not a finding. A finding that cannot be communicated is wasted. The synthesis is the bridge between the two.*
