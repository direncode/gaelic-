## Pattern Axes

### Origin
> *The Gaels originate in the ancient east — Scythia, Egypt, and the lands between Babel and Iberia — and arrive at Ireland through a long westward migration.*
>
> Source: *Lebor Gabála Érenn*, Milesian genealogy (Macalister ed., passim).

### Journey
> *The Milesian journey is explicitly staged: Babel → Scythia → Egypt → Crete → Sicily → Iberia → Ireland. The westward direction is the trajectory of legitimacy; eastern origin is the credential.*
>
> Source: LGE direct quote: *"...how they came from Egypt, and out of Scythia to the Maeotic Marshes, and along the Tyrrhene Sea to Crete and to Sicily; and we have further related how they took Spain by force."*

### Claim
> *Ireland is claimed by the poet Amergin through speech, not by warriors through battle. The Song of Amergin ("I am Wind on Sea, I am Ocean-wave...") is the act that transforms the Milesian landing from invasion into belonging.*
>
> Source: LGE, Song of Amergin at Inber Scéne.

### Name
> *Naming is covenant. Amergin promises Ériu that the island will bear her name — from this act, Éire. Names bind the namer and the named in a permanent relationship.*
>
> Source: LGE, the three sovereignty goddesses at Uisneach.

### Season
> *Samhain (November 1, eve October 31) is the liminal threshold. Major mythological events — invasions, Otherworld crossings, heroic arrivals — occur at Samhain.*
>
> Source: Táin Bó Cúailnge (invasion of Ulster begins at Samhain); Echtra Nerai (Cruachan Otherworld crossing); Tochmarc Étaíne (Midir reclaims Étaín at Samhain); Togail Bruidne Da Derga (Conaire Mór's death).

### Landscape
> *Connacht — specifically the Mayo/Galway borderlands — is the mythological heartland. Mag Tuired (battles of the Tuatha Dé Danann), Cruachan (Queen Medb's seat and Otherworld entrance), Loch Corrib (Manannán mac Lir associations), Clonfert (St. Brendan's immram monastery) all cluster here.*
>
> Source: Cath Maige Tuired; Metrical Dindsenchas (various Connacht entries); Navigatio Sancti Brendani.

### Diaspora
> *Gaelic identity operates on the principle of* **dúchas** *— heritage carried in lineage, not in soil. A person of Gaelic descent retains Gaelic identity regardless of birthplace. The LGE itself models this: the Milesians were non-Irish by birth for centuries of wandering, yet became the truest Irish through their lineage.*
>
> Source: Traditional Gaelic concept; McCone, *Pagan Past and Christian Present*.

### Lineage
> *Identity is transmitted through patronymic chains: Mac [X], Ó [X], Ní [X]. The Gaelic name is a kinship-declaration. The LGE's own genealogical spine runs through named fathers.*
>
> Source: Brehon law; patronymic tradition; LGE genealogical passages.

### Voice / Speech
> *The poet (fili) and the storyteller (seanchaí) are the carriers of identity. Their word has legal and sacral force. Amergin's poem defeats druidic sorcery; the seanchaí's tale carries the lineage across generations.*
>
> Source: Brehon laws on poetic status; Séamus Ó Duilearga, *The Gaelic Story-Teller*, Proceedings of the British Academy vol. 31, 1945.

## Declarative Naming Practice

Gaelic names are **kinship-declarations**. The patronymic structure (*Mac [father], Ó [grandfather], Ní [for women]*) makes every personal name a sentence about lineage. First names often carry meanings (*Cathal* = "battle-ruler"; *Aodh* = "fire"; *Brigid* = "high one / the exalted"). Surnames often include epithets (*Ruadh* = "red"; *Bán* = "fair / white"; *Dubh* = "dark").

The Gaelic tradition treats the name as **the form in which belonging is made audible**. To be named in Gaelic is to be placed in a chain that runs backward to named ancestors and forward to unnamed descendants.

## Diaspora Doctrine

Gaelic tradition does not explicitly articulate a vadzimu-like doctrine of travelling ancestors, but it operates on *dúchas* — the principle that heritage is carried in the blood, not in the ground. The LGE is itself a **proof-of-concept for this doctrine**: the Gaels are Gaels precisely because they carried their identity across centuries of migration through non-Irish lands before reaching Ireland.

For the engine, this means a Gaelic-heritage subject born in diaspora is not disqualified. The LGE's own story is the story of eastern-born, lineage-defined Irishness. Diaspora is the default Gaelic condition; the brief period of territorial concentration in Ireland is the exception.
