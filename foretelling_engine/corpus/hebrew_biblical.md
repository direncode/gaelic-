## Pattern Axes

### Origin
Abraham's call from Ur (*"lech lecha me-artzecha"* — Genesis 12:1: "Go forth from your land"). The Israelite origin is itself an eastern-origin narrative: Mesopotamia → Canaan. The Egyptian sojourn and Exodus reinforce the migration pattern.

### Journey
Explicit and foundational: the 40 years of wilderness wandering; Exile to Babylon; Return; the Second Temple destruction and dispersion; the modern return. Migration and return are the Jewish grand narrative.

### Claim
The Land of Israel is claimed through divine covenant (*brit*), not conquest: promise to Abraham (Gen 15, 17), ratified at Sinai. Naming and covenant are parallel mechanisms.

### Name
**Theophoric names are central.** The tetragrammaton (YHWH) and the generic *El* appear in countless compound names: *Yeshayahu, Yirmiyahu, Yehoshua, Yonatan, Yosef, Eliyahu, Daniel, Yechezkel, Natanel, Gavriel, Michael*. Virtue and circumstance names also common (*Rachel* = ewe; *Leah* = weary; *Barak* = lightning).

The practice of giving Hebrew names at circumcision/naming (*brit milah, simchat bat*) is universal and explicitly declarative.

### Season
Lunar calendar with significant holy days: Rosh Hashanah (new year), Yom Kippur, Pesach (Passover), Shavuot, Sukkot, Chanukah, Purim. Birth dates can carry meaning relative to Jewish calendar dates.

### Landscape
Eretz Yisrael; Jerusalem; the wilderness of Sinai; the specific towns of biblical narrative.

### Diaspora
**The most theologically elaborated diaspora doctrine of any tradition.** *Galut* (exile) is a core category. Jewish identity was explicitly designed to survive dispersion through text, ritual, and naming. Kabbalah interprets the diaspora as cosmic scattering awaiting reunification (*tikkun*).

### Lineage
Patrilineal for tribal affiliation; matrilineal for Jewish status (halakhic rule). Genealogy extensively recorded in biblical texts and post-biblical family trees.

### Voice
The Torah-reader (*ba'al koreh*), the cantor (*chazzan*), the darshan (preacher), the sofer (scribe) — all transmitters of identity through voice and text.

## Declarative Naming Practice

Hebrew names are among the most declarative in any tradition. Every name is typically a full theological or circumstantial statement. The giving of a Hebrew name at *brit* is itself a covenantal act, linking the child to the lineage.

Sephardic naming traditions honour living relatives; Ashkenazic traditions honour deceased relatives — each with its own logic of lineage-continuity.

## Diaspora Doctrine

The Jewish people are **the diaspora tradition par excellence**. Survival across two millennia of dispersion depended on the transmission of names, texts, and ritual. For engine queries on Jewish-heritage subjects born anywhere in the global diaspora, the tradition's position is: *the identity travels with the name, the text, and the calendar*.
