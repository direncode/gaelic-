## Pattern Axes

### Origin
Yoruba tradition traces origin to **Ilé-Ifè** — the sacred city where creation occurred. Oduduwa descended from the heavens (or arrived from the east) and founded the city. All Yoruba kingdoms trace descent to Ilé-Ifè.

### Journey
Migration from Ilé-Ifè outward established the kingdoms of Oyo, Ibadan, Abeokuta, Ijebu, Ondo, and the broader Yoruba world.

### Claim
Divine descent from Oduduwa; the *ade* (beaded crown) as symbol of legitimate sovereignty.

### Name
**Names are essential messages.** Yoruba naming is among the most declarative in any tradition catalogued. Three main types:
- **Orúko àmútọ̀runwá** — "name brought from heaven" (birth-circumstance names: Taiwo, Kehinde for twins; Idowu for child born after twins)
- **Orúko àbísọ** — "name given at birth" (declarative names with specific meanings)
- **Oríkì** — praise names recited by family to honour the child, accumulated across life

A Yoruba person often has multiple names; each carries a message.

### Season
Ifá divination operates on the *odu* system rather than solar calendar. Specific festivals (Egungun, Ọbatala, Ọṣun) are traditionally significant.

### Landscape
Ilé-Ifè; the individual town's sacred grove; the king's (oba's) palace; rivers (especially Ọṣun at Osogbo).

### Diaspora
Massive diaspora via the Atlantic slave trade produced Candomblé (Brazil), Santería (Cuba), Lucumí, Vodou (Haitian syncretism). The Yoruba tradition is the most robust diaspora tradition in the Americas — names, deities (orishas), praise forms all survived.

### Lineage
Matrilineal in some contexts, patrilineal in others. *Idile* (lineage) and compound (*agbo ilé*) structure identity.

### Voice
The *babaláwo* (Ifá priest) recites the Odu. Women recite *oríkì* over children and kin. Praise is the mechanism of identity transmission.

## Declarative Naming Practice

Yoruba names commonly include meaningful elements:
- *Olú-* ("lord, chief")
- *Adé-* ("crown")
- *Ọlá-* ("wealth, honour")
- *Ifá-* ("Ifá, divination")
- *Ọṣun-* / *Ṣàngó-* / *Ògún-* (theophoric, naming after orishas)
- *-tunde* ("returned/reborn")
- *-wale* ("come home")

A full Yoruba name is typically a sentence about the child, their circumstances, or the family's hope.

## Diaspora Doctrine

Yoruba tradition has been empirically *proven* to travel: surviving as a complete ritual and onomastic system across the Atlantic, in Brazil (Candomblé), Cuba (Santería/Lucumí), Haiti (Vodou syncretism), and Trinidad. The orishas remained named and worshipped; praise names remained intact.

For engine queries on people of Yoruba diaspora heritage (especially African American, Afro-Caribbean, Afro-Brazilian), the tradition's demonstrated diaspora persistence is a key pattern axis.
