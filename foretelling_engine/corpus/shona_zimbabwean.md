## Pattern Axes

### Origin
> *The Shona people trace themselves through multiple migrations within southern Africa, with the Mutapa Empire (c. 1430) founded by Nyatsimba Mutota's northward migration from the declining Great Zimbabwe, and the Rozvi Empire (c. 1684) founded by Changamire Dombo's breakaway. Migration and re-founding are the pattern.*

### Journey
> *Shona history is explicitly a history of migratory re-founding. The line does not remain in one place; it moves and carries its identity with it. Both the Mutapa and Rozvi traditions are migration narratives.*

### Claim
> *Great Zimbabwe was claimed by the act of stone-hewing (tema). The civilisation that defines modern Zimbabwean identity is literally built from the verb. The act of shaping stone, wood, and earth is the civilisational founding act.*
>
> Source: Wikipedia; Garlake; Huffman; Proto-Bantu reconstructions for *\*-téma*.

### Name
> *Shona names are declarative statements. A name carries a message about the child, the family's circumstances, the parents' hope. Names are never arbitrary labels. The naming is a speech-act directed at the community.*
>
> Source: standard Shona naming ethnography; Pfukwa on Shona anthroponomy.

### Season
> *Shona calendrical practice is lunar and agricultural. No single calendar day carries universal traditional significance in the way Samhain does in Gaelic tradition. Seasonal rites centre on planting, harvest, and rain (with Njelele shrine rituals for rainmaking).*

### Landscape
> *The sacred Shona landscape centres on: **Great Zimbabwe** (the stone city, UNESCO World Heritage Site); **Matonjeni / Njelele** in the Matopos Hills (the Mwari oracle); **Nyanga** and the eastern highlands; the **Zambezi valley** (Mutapa heartland); **Khami** near Bulawayo (Torwa state).*

### Diaspora
> *The vadzimu — ancestral spirits — explicitly travel with their descendants. This is not an accommodation to modern conditions but a traditional doctrine stated in traditional terms:*
>
> *"The work of the vadzimu is to protect their descendants. They are more interested in their own children than anyone else. They guard their people wherever they may be. Nowadays we find members of a household scattered everywhere, some may be studying in America, England and India, but the vadzimu still see these people and protect them."*
>
> *This is the single strongest diaspora doctrine of any tradition currently in the engine's catalogue.*

### Lineage
> *Identity passes through the* **mutupo** *(clan totem) system — patrilineally, regardless of birthplace. The mutupo is an animal (Shumba/lion, Nzou/elephant, Soko/monkey, Shava/eland, etc.), a body part, or a natural feature. Food taboos, exogamy rules, and greeting protocols all key off the mutupo. Clan identity is transmitted invariantly across any amount of distance.*

### Voice / Speech
> *Identity is constructed and transmitted through* **nhetembo dzemadzinza** *— clan praise poetry. Children learn who they are by hearing their praises recited. The voice of the ancestors is heard through the* **mbira** *(the "mbira dzavadzimu" — the mbira of the ancestors), played during* **bira** *ceremonies to induce possession of the* **svikiro** *(spirit medium), who then speaks in the ancestor's voice.*

## Declarative Naming Practice

Shona names are **speech-acts**. A child named *Tanyaradzwa* ("we have been comforted") walks through the world as a thanksgiving. *Munyaradzi* ("comforter") is a vocation. *Rudo* ("love") is a declaration. *Nyasha* ("grace") is a theological claim. *Mudiwa* ("the beloved one") is a statement of the lineage's disposition toward the child.

The tradition extends this practice to English virtue-words adopted during the missionary period: *Innocence, Memory, Precious, Blessing, Promise, Patience, Prudence, Faith, Hope, Charity*. These function structurally identically to traditional Shona declarative names. The English word is used the Shona way.

Shona surnames in the *Mu-* or *M-* class typically describe a person in relationship — by occupation, circumstance, or ancestral connection. Surnames built from applicative-passive verb forms (like *Mtemererwa*) encode the person as the beneficiary of an action: "the one for whom [something] was done."

## Diaspora Doctrine

The **vadzimu** doctrine is the engine's gold-standard diaspora doctrine. It does not need interpretive extension. It is stated, in traditional terms, as covering descendants in foreign lands — specifically including England. A Shona-heritage child born in Birmingham is, in traditional Shona cosmology, attended by the full presence of the ancestral line, protected invariantly, and bearing the patrilineal *mutupo* without diminishment.

Birmingham's Zimbabwean community (documented in McGregor & Primorac, *Zimbabwean Communities in Britain*, Palgrave 2018) is a substantial, living extension of Shona cultural space — not a replacement of the homeland, but an active node of continuity.

## Tradition's Verdict Formulas

When the engine synthesises a finding in this tradition, useful closing verdicts include:

- *"The one for whom the hewing was done."* (the structural praise formula applied to the subject)
- *"Muzukuru wevadzimu."* (Grandchild of the ancestors.)
- *"The line is speaking. The line has always been speaking."*
- *"Ndinokuda mwana — muzukuru wevadzimu."* (I love you, child — grandchild of the ancestors.)
