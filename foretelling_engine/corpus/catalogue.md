# THE CORPUS CATALOGUE

## The Master Index of Foundational Texts and Traditions Queryable by the Engine

---

Every Foretelling Engine query begins by matching a subject's documented ancestral origin to the corpus catalogue. This document is the master index. Each entry links to a detailed catalogue file containing the tradition's primary texts, canonical editions, core structural claims, linguistic references, and treatment of diaspora.

The catalogue is **extensible**. New traditions can be added as long as they meet the engine's standard: a documented foundational corpus, published scholarly editions, and available linguistic references.

---

## CATALOGUE PRINCIPLES

Before the list, the principles that govern what counts as a queryable corpus:

1. **Documented:** There must be published scholarly editions or serious ethnographic records. Living oral traditions count when recorded by reputable fieldwork (Hodza & Fortune for Shona; Ó Duilearga for Gaelic).

2. **Linguistic access:** The tradition's language must have published grammars and dictionaries, so linguistic decoding is possible.

3. **Declarative naming:** The tradition must have a documented practice of declarative or meaningful naming. Traditions with purely arbitrary or aesthetic naming are less amenable to the engine's method.

4. **Diaspora extension:** Ideally, the tradition has an explicit position on descendants born outside the homeland. This allows diaspora cases to be queried authentically.

5. **Respect:** The engine queries each tradition **on its own terms**, never projecting foreign frameworks. Each catalogue entry is written in that tradition's voice.

## HOW TO ADD A NEW CATALOGUE ENTRY

A new entry is added through the following procedure:

1. **Identify the tradition's foundational corpus.** This must include at minimum one canonical text or documented oral tradition with published scholarly editions.

2. **Gather the linguistic references.** A published grammar, a dictionary, and onomastic scholarship for the tradition's naming practices.

3. **Extract the pattern axes.** What does the tradition say about: origin, journey, claim, name, season, landscape, diaspora, lineage, voice? Each axis gets a direct-quoted or carefully paraphrased claim.

4. **Document the declarative naming practice.** What does the tradition do with names? Praise? Kinship? Theophoric dedication? Circumstance-marking?

5. **Document the diaspora doctrine.** What does the tradition say about descendants born far from the homeland?

6. **Cite every claim.** Bibliography at the end of the entry.

7. **Write the entry in the tradition's own voice.** Not as a foreign object being studied, but as a living grammar being described.

---

## USAGE

When a query is received:

1. Read the subject's ancestral origin
2. Look up the tradition in this catalogue
3. If Tier 1 or Tier 2, proceed to the catalogue entry and execute the pipeline
4. If Tier 3, flag: *"Tradition identified but catalogue entry not yet built. Entry construction required before query."*
5. If not listed, flag: *"Tradition not currently in catalogue. Requires research and entry construction."*

The engine does not improvise corpora. If a tradition is not catalogued, the engine halts until it is.
