## Pattern Axes

### Origin
The Æsir are traced (in Snorri's euhemeristic frame) from Troy (*Tyrkland*) via "Asia" — another eastern-origin tradition. The Norse claim of descent from eastern sources parallels the Milesian pattern structurally.

### Journey
Viking and Settlement Age migrations. *Landnámabók* records the westward settlement of Iceland, a precise parallel to the westward Gaelic migration narrative. The *landnám* (land-taking) is the foundational act.

### Claim
Land claimed through naming, circumambulation, fire, and formal declaration before witnesses. *Landnáma* records the specific rituals.

### Name
Strong onomastic tradition. Names carry meaning (*Sigurðr* = "victory-guardian"; *Þórstein* = "Thor's stone"; *Guðrún* = "god-rune"). Bynames (*viðrnöfn*) describe character, appearance, deeds. Patronymics universal (*-son, -dóttir*).

### Season
*Yule* (midwinter) and the turning of the year are heavily charged. Samhain-cognate timings (blood-month, *blót*month).

### Landscape
Iceland and the specific settled fjords; Scandinavia proper; the *útgarð* (outer world) as threshold.

### Diaspora
Strong — the entire Icelandic identity is a diaspora identity, constructed through the *landnám* memory. Descendants across the North Atlantic remained Norse in their naming and memory for centuries.

### Lineage
Patronymic system extends back through named ancestors; the sagas are effectively genealogical texts with narrative embedding.

### Voice
The skald (court poet) and the *sögumaðr* (saga-teller) preserve identity through formal verse and prose.

## Declarative Naming Practice

Norse names are **meaning-carrying** and often theophoric (*Þór-* elements especially). Patronymics universal. Bynames highly specific. A Norse name is a sentence about the bearer's nature, ancestry, or reputation.

## Diaspora Doctrine

The Icelandic settlement is itself a diaspora foundation — *Landnámabók* is a diaspora text. The tradition treats lineage-preservation across migration as the norm.
