### Morphological Parse — Mtemererwa

| Morpheme | Function | Meaning | Source |
|----------|----------|---------|--------|
| *M-* | class 1 personal prefix (variant of *Mu-*) | "the one who..." | Fortune 1955 |
| *-tem-* | verb root from *-tema* | "to cut, hew, carve, strike with a blade" | Hannan; Proto-Bantu *\*-téma* (BLR) |
| *-er-* | applicative suffix | "for, on behalf of, at" | Fortune 1955 |
| *-w-* | passive suffix | "is being... / has been..." | Fortune 1955 |
| *-a* | final vowel | (grammatical closure) | Shona phonotactics |

**Rendered:** *"The one for whom the cutting/hewing was done"* — "the beneficiary of the stone-hewing."

### Proto-Form Reconstruction
- *tema* is inherited from Proto-Bantu *\*-téma*, reconstructed from a language spoken approximately 3,000–4,000 years ago in West-Central Africa. This places a verb of civilisational depth at the core of the surname.

### Combined Meaning
- **Literal:** *"The-one-loved, the-one-for-whom-cutting-was-done."*
- **Rendered:** ***"The Beloved One for Whose Sake the Stones Were Hewn."***

### Position in Tradition's Naming Grammar
Shona names are declarative speech-acts. *Mudiwa* is a direct declarative ("this child is beloved"). *Mtemererwa* is an applicative-passive surname, grammatically marking the bearer as the beneficiary of an action. This is a specific and unusual construction — not "the cutter" (active) or "the cut one" (simple passive), but "the one for whom the cutting was done" (applicative-passive).

### Civilisational Resonances
- **tema** is the verb that built **Great Zimbabwe** — the dry-stone city whose name the modern country bears. Every stone in the Great Enclosure (walls up to 11 metres high, 13th–14th century, no mortar) was shaped by this verb.
- Adding the applicative-passive construction to this verb makes the surname a grammatical **dedication** — the stones were cut *for* the bearer.

### Multi-Bearer Lineage Observations (CRITICAL)

Combined readings across three known bearers:

- **George Mtemererwa** — "The earth-worker for whom the hewing was done."
- **Innocence Mtemererwa** — "The pure one for whom the hewing was done."
- **Mudiwa Mtemererwa** — "The beloved one for whom the hewing was done."

Read as a **lineage-wide praise declaration**, this sequence performs precisely the structure of Shona *nhetembo dzemadzinza*: a constant ancestral claim (the surname) with varying first-name epithets marking each descendant's specific relationship to the line. The family is actively transmitting a coherent declarative naming pattern across generations in the diaspora.
