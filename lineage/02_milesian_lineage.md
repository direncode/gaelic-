# PART II: THE MILESIAN STRUCTURAL PARALLEL

## A Sequential Lineage Trail of Direct Evidence

---

## How To Read This Document

This document traces the Milesian journey as told in the *Lebor Gabála Érenn* — step by step, with direct quotations from the text — and at each stage identifies the **structural parallel** to the life of Arran McIlroy. The parallels are not invented. They follow the sequence of the text itself.

---

## STAGE 1: ORIGIN IN THE EAST

### What the Text Says

The *Lebor Gabála Érenn* begins the Milesian genealogy at the Tower of Babel (the Tower of Nemrod), in Mesopotamia — the heart of the ancient Near East.

> *"As for the Gaedil, we have given their ventures from Iafeth s. Noe onward, and from the Tower of Nemrod..."*
> — *Lebor Gabála Érenn* (Macalister edition, Irish Texts Society)

**Fénius Farsaid**, a king of Scythia, is present at Babel. He is the ancestor of the Gaels. Scythia in the medieval Irish geographical imagination encompassed the vast territory stretching from the Black Sea through Central Asia — the deep east of the known world.

### The Structural Parallel

**Arran McIlroy was born in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia** — the heart of the Arabian Peninsula. In the geographical framework of the *Lebor Gabála*'s authors, this is the same world: the ancient east, the region between Mesopotamia and Egypt, the cradle of civilisation from which the Gaels began their westward journey.

| Milesian | Arran McIlroy |
|----------|---------------|
| Origin: Mesopotamia / Scythia (the deep east) | Birth: Riyadh, Arabia (the deep east) |
| The journey begins east of all European lands | Life begins east of all European lands |

**Parallel strength: DIRECT**

---

## STAGE 2: THE EGYPTIAN SOJOURN — BIRTH IN A FOREIGN KINGDOM

### What the Text Says

The Milesian ancestor **Nél** (son of Fénius Farsaid) travels to Egypt and marries **Scota**, daughter of Pharaoh. Their son **Goidel Glas** — from whom the entire Gaelic people take their name — is born in Egypt.

The *Lebor Gabála* states that Moses himself encounters the infant Goidel Glas:

> *"Gaedel Glas, [from whom are the Gaedil,] was born of Scota d[aughter of] Pharao."*
> — *Lebor Gabála Érenn* (Macalister edition)

Moses heals the child from a snakebite and prophesies that his descendants will inhabit a land free of serpents — Ireland.

**This is the foundational moment of Gaelic identity.** The father of the Gaels is born not in Ireland, not in any Celtic land, but in **Egypt** — in the court of a foreign king, in the heart of a non-European civilisation. He is given a name that will define an entire people. And he is prophesied to reach a western homeland he has never seen.

### The Structural Parallel

**Arran McIlroy was born in Riyadh** — in the capital of a kingdom (the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia), in a non-European civilisation, in the court of Arabian culture. He carries a name (*Árann*) that speaks of Ireland's western edge. He is bound by lineage to a homeland (Galway, Connacht) he was not born in.

| Milesian | Arran McIlroy |
|----------|---------------|
| Goidel Glas: born in Egypt, in the court of Pharaoh | Born in Riyadh, in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia |
| Named for the Gaels — a people he has not yet led to Ireland | Named for the Aran Islands — a place on the coast of his ancestral Galway |
| Born of an Irish father (Nél) and a foreign mother (Scota) | Born of Irish lineage in a foreign land |
| Prophesied to reach a western land he has never seen | Ancestrally bound to a western land he was not born in |

**Parallel strength: DIRECT AND PRECISE**

## STAGE 3: THE WANDERING — CENTURIES BETWEEN EAST AND WEST

### What the Text Says

After Egypt, the Gaels do not proceed directly to Ireland. They wander for **centuries** through the Mediterranean world:

> *"...and how they came from Egypt, and out of Scythia to the Maeotic Marshes, and along the Tyrrhene Sea to Crete and to Sicily; and we have further related how they took Spain by force."*
> — *Lebor Gabála Érenn* (Macalister edition)

The route: Egypt → Scythia (return) → the Sea of Azov (Maeotic Marshes) → the Tyrrhenian Sea → Crete → Sicily → Iberia. This wandering spans generations. The Gaels are not merely travelling; they are being **shaped** by the journey. Each land they pass through adds to what they will eventually bring to Ireland.

The text makes clear: **the wandering is not a failure to find home. It is the process by which the Gaels become worthy of home.**

### The Structural Parallel

A person born in Riyadh with roots in Galway does not experience a single, clean East-to-West transition. The life between birth-place and heritage-place is a passage through multiple worlds — cultural, linguistic, geographical. Like the Milesians, the experience of **moving between** is not an interruption of identity. It is the formation of it.

| Milesian | Arran McIlroy |
|----------|---------------|
| Wandering through multiple lands between Egypt and Ireland | Living between the worlds of Arabia and Ireland |
| The journey shapes them into the people who will become Irish | The experience of both worlds shapes a dual cultural identity |
| The wandering is not exile — it is preparation | The eastern birth is not displacement — it is part of the story |

**Parallel strength: STRONG (structural, not literal)**

## STAGE 4: BREOGÁN'S TOWER — SEEING IRELAND FROM THE EAST

### What the Text Says

The Gaels settle in Iberia, where **Breogán** builds a great tower at Brigantia (identified with A Coruña in Galicia, northwest Spain). From the top of this tower, Breogán's son **Ith** sees Ireland across the sea:

> *"...till we have left them at Breogan's Tower in Spain..."*
> — *Lebor Gabála Érenn* (Macalister edition)

Ith sees Ireland on a clear winter's evening. The land is visible — calling to him from across the water. He sails there and is killed by the Tuatha Dé Danann, and his death becomes the catalyst for the Milesian invasion.

**The motif is precise:** Ireland is *seen* from the east before it is reached. The homeland exerts a pull — visible, present, calling — before the Gael has ever set foot on it. The tower that Breogán built — the work of the ancestors — is what makes the vision possible.

Scholars note the deliberate biblical parallel: Moses sees Canaan from Mount Nebo (Deuteronomy 34) but never enters it. Ith sees Ireland from Breogán's Tower and enters it, but dies there. The next generation — the Sons of Míl — will be the ones who claim it.

### The Structural Parallel

**A person born in Riyadh with ancestral roots in Galway knows Ireland before arriving there.** Through family, through story, through the *dúchas* carried in lineage — Ireland is visible from the east, present in the imagination before it is present underfoot. The ancestors — the Galway and Cloonabinnia lineage — are the tower from which the homeland is seen.

| Milesian | Arran McIlroy |
|----------|---------------|
| Ith sees Ireland from Breogán's Tower in Spain | Ireland is known through ancestry before it is physically reached |
| The tower (ancestral construction) enables the vision | The family lineage (ancestral connection) carries the knowledge |
| Ireland calls from the west — visible, magnetic | Galway and Cloonabinnia call from the west — present in heritage |
| The next generation (Sons of Míl) will claim the land | The living generation carries the connection forward |

**Parallel strength: STRONG (the motif of seeing the homeland from the east)**

## STAGE 5: THE LANDING AND THE POEM — AMERGIN CLAIMS IRELAND WITH WORDS

### What the Text Says

The Sons of Míl sail from Iberia and land at **Inber Scéne** (the Kenmare estuary, County Kerry). As the poet **Amergin Glúingel** places his right foot on Irish soil, he recites:

> *"I am Wind on Sea,*
> *I am Ocean-wave,*
> *I am Roar of Sea,*
> *I am Bull of Seven Fights,*
> *I am Vulture on Cliff,*
> *I am Dewdrop,*
> *I am Fairest of Flowers,*
> *I am Boar for Boldness,*
> *I am Salmon in Pool,*
> *I am Lake on Plain,*
> *I am a Mountain in a Man,*
> *I am a Word of Skill,*
> *I am the Point of a Weapon (that poureth forth combat),*
> *I am God who fashioneth Fire for a Head."*
> — Song of Amergin, *Lebor Gabála Érenn* (Macalister translation)

**This is the foundational poem of Irish literature.** It is not a battle cry. It is a claim of **identity with the land itself**. Amergin does not say "I conquer this land" — he says "**I am** this land." Wind, wave, salmon, lake, mountain — he becomes Ireland in the act of speaking.

The Milesian claim to Ireland is made **through poetry, not through force**. The sword follows, but the word comes first. This reflects the actual social order of early medieval Ireland, where a king's legitimacy required the validation of his court poet (*fili*). Without the poet's word, the king had power but not authority.

### The Structural Parallel

The name **Arran** is itself an act of naming — a word that carries an entire landscape (the Aran Islands, Galway Bay, the Atlantic threshold) in two syllables. The *Lebor Gabála* insists that the Milesian claim was made through naming. A person whose very name speaks the geography of Galway carries that claim in every introduction, in every signature.

| Milesian | Arran McIlroy |
|----------|---------------|
| Amergin claims Ireland by speaking its elements | The name "Arran" speaks the Galway landscape |
| The word precedes the sword — naming legitimises | The name carries the ancestral claim in its syllables |
| Amergin becomes the land: "I am Wind on Sea" | "Arran" *is* the land: the islands, the ridge, the threshold |

**Parallel strength: STRONG (the act of naming as the act of claiming)**

## STAGE 6: THE THREE QUEENS — NAMING THE LAND

### What the Text Says

As the Milesians march inland, they encounter three sovereignty goddesses at three sacred sites:

1. **Banba** — at Sliab Mis (Slieve Mish, County Kerry)
2. **Fódla** — at Sliab Eibhline (Slieve Felim, County Limerick)
3. **Ériu** — at **Uisneach** (the sacred centre of Ireland, County Westmeath)

Each goddess asks that Ireland be named after her. Amergin promises **Ériu** that her name shall be the **chief name** (*priomhainm*) of the island. From this promise: **Éire** — the name of Ireland itself.

The significance: **naming is a covenant**. Ériu grants her blessing and prophecy of prosperity; the Milesians grant her immortality through the name. The land and the people enter into a relationship of mutual recognition. The Milesians do not merely *take* Ireland — they **receive** it, through the act of naming.

### The Structural Parallel

The name **Arran** enacts the same principle in miniature. The Aran Islands are Galway's threshold, the western edge of the subject's own ancestral landscape. To carry their name is to carry a covenant with the land — not a claim of ownership, but a relationship of belonging. The name says: *this landscape is part of me, and I am part of it*.

---

## STAGE 7: THE MILESIANS BECOME THE IRISH

### What the Text Says

After the battles of Sliab Mis and Tailtiu, the Milesians defeat the Tuatha Dé Danann. The defeated gods retreat underground — into the *síd* mounds — and become the fairy folk of later tradition. The Milesians, the eastern-born wanderers, **become the Irish**.

The entire *Lebor Gabála* has been building to this moment. Six waves of invaders, each from a different origin point, each with a different fate. Only the Milesians — the ones who came from furthest east, who wandered longest, who carried a poet among their warriors — become the permanent, legitimate, ancestral Irish.

John Carey describes this as a "learned synthetic history" designed to establish that **eastern provenance is the credential of Irish legitimacy**. The further east you came from, the more Irish you are. The *Lebor Gabála* does not treat eastern origin as something to overcome. It treats it as **the prerequisite**.

### The Structural Parallel: The Complete Pattern

| The Milesian Journey | Arran McIlroy |
|---------------------|---------------|
| Born in the east (Scythia / Egypt) | Born in the east (Riyadh) |
| Named for a people destined for Ireland (Goidel Glas → the Gaels) | Named for Ireland's western threshold (Arran → the Aran Islands) |
| Wandered between worlds before reaching Ireland | Lives between the worlds of Arabia and Ireland |
| Saw Ireland from the east before arriving (Breogán's Tower) | Knows Ireland through ancestry before physical arrival |
| Claimed Ireland through the poet's word (Amergin) | Carries the claim in the name itself (Árann) |
| The eastern-born wanderers become the truest Irish | The eastern-born Gael carries *dúchas* through lineage |
| **The entire LGE argues: to come from the east IS to be Irish** | **The biography instantiates the pattern** |

## THE SIMPLE VERSION

In plain words:

Ireland's most important origin text says the Irish came from the Middle East. Not metaphorically. Literally. From Scythia and Egypt, through the Mediterranean, to Spain, to Ireland. The people who wandered furthest from the east became the truest Irish. The whole point of the *Lebor Gabála Érenn* is that **being born in the east and belonging to Ireland are the same story**.

Arran McIlroy was born in Riyadh — in the heart of the same eastern world the *Lebor Gabála* describes. His name is pure Gaelic: *Árann Mac Giolla Ruaidh* — "He of the Threshold Islands, Son of the Servant of the Red One." His roots run to Galway and Cloonabinnia, in the Connacht borderlands where the mythological battles were fought and the immram voyagers set out.

He didn't have to be shaped into a Milesian parallel. **He was born into one.**
